AI Demand Forecasting for Fashion Retail

August 11, 2026 · 2 min read
Integrated Retail
Retail Technology Team

** AI demand forecasting for fashion retail predicts sell-through at the level where fashion is actually bought: store × product × size × color. That granularity is what turns a forecast from a finance report into an operational tool — one that tells you which size curve will break in which store, weeks before it happens.

Why fashion demand is genuinely hard to forecast​

  • Short seasons.** By the time a traditional forecast has enough history to be confident, the selling window is half over.

  • Size curves. Companywide the item looks fine; in a specific store, the two sizes that sell are gone and the rest is dead stock.

  • Intermittent demand. Many SKU-store pairs sell zero units most weeks — averages mislead, and naive models either overstock or give up.

  • Location personality. The same dress behaves differently in a mall flagship, an outlet, and a resort store.

What AI does differently​

The practical advance is not one clever model — it is automatic model selection per SKU and store. Seasonal items get seasonal treatment, trending items get momentum-aware models, and intermittent sellers get models built for sparse demand. No planner has to tune anything by hand, and the forecast updates with every sell-through cycle.

Size curve analysis: the fashion-specific win​

The highest-value output for apparel teams is usually the size curve view: comparing actual sales, the ideal curve, and stock on hand per store. That comparison surfaces broken size runs while there is still time to fix them — and a broken size run found early is a transfer, not a markdown.

From forecast to action​

A forecast only earns its keep when it changes a decision. Granular demand forecasts feed directly into inter-store transfer optimization — pairing predicted shortage with existing excess — and into replenishment priorities, so the units you already own end up where they will sell.
